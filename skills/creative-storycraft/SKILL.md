# Skill: Creative Storycraft

## Goal
Turn a story intent into a coherent narrative artifact: premise, world, characters, outline, scene, chapter, script, or revision plan.

## Use This Skill When
- The user asks for story writing, fiction, screenplay, interactive narrative, lore, worldbuilding, characters, plot, dialogue, or prose revision.
- The user wants a reusable story bible, beat sheet, chapter plan, or continuity pass.
- The user gives only a mood, image, song, or symbolic seed and wants it manifested as narrative.

## Do Not Use This Skill When
- The user asks for factual reporting or biography; use research/verification skills first.
- The user wants only grammar cleanup with no creative development.
- The request would imitate a living author's style too closely; transform into neutral craft attributes instead.

## Inputs
- Form: short story, novel chapter, screenplay, comic script, game quest, lore document, etc.
- Target audience, rating, length, genre, POV, tense, voice, and constraints.
- Seed material: premise, theme, characters, setting, conflict, mood, references.

## Workflow
1. **Extract the dramatic contract**
   - State protagonist, desire, obstacle, stakes, wound/flaw, pressure clock, and irreversible choice.
   - Name the theme as a question, not a slogan.
2. **Build the story spine**
   - Produce logline, promise-of-genre, act/beat outline, escalation ladder, and ending image.
   - Track cause/effect; avoid disconnected cool moments.
3. **Create or update the story bible**
   - Characters: want, need, lie, contradiction, voice markers, relationships.
   - World: rules, costs, taboos, sensory palette, social forces.
   - Continuity: dates, locations, powers/tools, unresolved setups.
4. **Draft scenes as units of change**
   - For each scene define entry state, conflict turn, reveal/reversal, exit state, and hook.
   - Prefer concrete action, sensory detail, and subtext over exposition.
5. **Revise in passes**
   - Macro: structure, stakes, pacing, arc.
   - Meso: scene goal/conflict/turn, continuity, transitions.
   - Micro: line rhythm, image systems, dialogue, redundancy.
6. **Record open questions**
   - Separate facts established in draft from provisional options.

## Output
- The requested story artifact.
- A compact continuity ledger when the artifact changes canon.
- Optional next-scene or next-revision recommendations.

## Quality Checks
- Every major beat changes a value: safety, intimacy, power, truth, status, or time.
- Character choices create consequences; coincidences only complicate.
- Exposition arrives under pressure or through conflict.
- The ending pays off the opening promise without erasing cost.
